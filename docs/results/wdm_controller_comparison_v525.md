# Paired simulated WDM controller comparison (v5.25)

## Question and evidence boundary

This campaign asks whether the existing WDM-only EdgeLSTM controllers improve
an operational admission outcome over simple baselines when every controller
sees the same held-out simulated trace. It does **not** test measured WDM against
a measured quantum target. All fidelities and WDM inputs in this experiment are
generated by `QuantumNetworkDatasetV3`, and no physical action is possible.

The predeclared configuration is
[`experiments/configs/wdm_controller_comparison_v525.json`](../../experiments/configs/wdm_controller_comparison_v525.json),
whose canonical SHA-256 is
`76d975cde902196af4d6a726158f8777974fdd4c6f9229bdb94c10fb4a899a7f`.
It fixes ten seeds, a temporal fit/calibration/test split, 100 paired cycles per
seed and regime, a fidelity threshold of 0.65, and a maximum permitted
prediction uncertainty of 0.20. No model or threshold was selected using the
test results.

## Compared controllers

| Controller | Information available at the decision |
|---|---|
| Persistence | Last simulated fidelity; calibration-tail RMS uncertainty |
| Reactive | Last simulated fidelity, treated as a current-state rule rather than a forecast |
| EdgeLSTM point | WDM-only window; calibration-tail RMS uncertainty |
| EdgeLSTM probabilistic | WDM-only window; learned sigma with calibration-tail temperature |
| EdgeLSTM DualHead | WDM-only window; availability gate plus conditional fidelity and calibration-tail RMS uncertainty |
| Oracle | Future simulated fidelity; upper-bound diagnostic, not a deployable controller |

Each seed uses the same held-out indices for all six controllers. The scaler is
fit only through the final training feature row. Two zero-shot secondary shifts
increase depolarization fivefold or double link distance. Results separate
prediction metrics, the HALT/WAIT/PURIFY admission scorecard, and an audit of
the existing four-action HALT/WAIT/PURIFY/SWAP sandbox policy.

## Registered primary result

The primary outcome was expected useful BBPSSW pairs per available-pair arrival
on the in-distribution held-out trace. The three neural controllers were each
compared with persistence using an exact paired seed-level sign-flip test, with
Holm correction across the three comparisons.

| Candidate versus persistence | Mean difference (percentage points) | Raw p | Holm p | Positive and family-wise significant |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| EdgeLSTM point | 0.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | No |
| EdgeLSTM probabilistic | 0.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | No |
| EdgeLSTM DualHead | 0.000 | 1.000 | 1.000 | No |

Result: **0/3 registered comparisons were positive and significant**. This is
not evidence of operational gain over persistence.

## Why the primary outcome was zero

The result is not an unexplained model tie. Honest calibration produced mean
uncertainties above the predeclared 0.20 safety ceiling for persistence and all
three neural controllers. Consequently the admission safety gate was active on
100% of available-pair cases and none of those four controllers executed a
purification in the evaluated trace.

| Persistence | 0.302 | 0.419 | 0.517 | 59.05 | 0.0 | 0.000 | 100.0 | 0.0% |
| Reactive | 0.302 | 0.000 | 0.165 | 0.00 | 1.8 | 1.998 | 0.0 | 0.0% |

| EdgeLSTM probabilistic | 0.293 | 0.395 | 0.591 | 100.00 | 0.0 | 0.000 | 100.0 | 54.0% |
| EdgeLSTM DualHead | 0.267 | 0.376 | 0.592 | 99.68 | 0.0 | 0.000 | 100.0 | 54.0% |
| Oracle | 0.000 | 0.000 | 1.000 | 0.00 | 3.0 | 3.302 | 0.0 | 0.0% |

The DualHead has the best descriptive MAE among the deployable predictors, but
that improvement does not become decision value under the frozen uncertainty
and safety rules. The 54% held-out OOD-window rate for all neural models is
also reported rather than hidden; it follows from applying training-only
feature bounds to complete temporal windows.

The reactive and oracle rows show that the analytical scorecard itself can
produce non-zero useful yield. They do not establish a WDM forecasting gain:
reactive uses privileged simulated target history, and the oracle directly uses
the future simulated target.

## Four-action sandbox policy audit

The unchanged `PolicyEngine` was independently evaluated at a fixed, declared
state (`STORING`, two available simulated pairs with fidelities 0.9 and 0.9).
Across 10 seeds and 6 controllers, all **60/60** in-distribution
controller-seed rows selected only one intended action: `SWAP`. Prediction
ranges differed substantially, but the action did not.

This exposes an action-space problem: purification and swapping are competing
as if they were interchangeable terminal operations, while the swap utility is
evaluated whenever a partner pair exists. Under the audited state, that utility
dominates the decision and masks predictor differences. Safety still changes
some effective actions to `HALT`, but a safety override is not evidence that the
predictor improved the intended policy.

## Scientific interpretation

The campaign adds a useful negative result and a falsification check:

- lower MAE alone is insufficient to claim operational controller value;
- calibrated uncertainty can make an otherwise promising predictor unusable
  under a fixed safety budget;
- an action policy can saturate and erase all sensitivity to its predictor;
- resource savings of 100% are meaningless when they arise from universal
  abstention and zero useful output.

The central thesis remains open because this campaign is simulated. The result
does not falsify the possibility that real synchronized WDM telemetry predicts
future measured QBER, SKR or Bell fidelity. It does falsify the narrower idea
that the current calibrated models and current four-action policy already
demonstrate deployable operational gain.

## Reproduction and artifacts

Run from the repository root:

```powershell
$env:PYTHONPATH = "$PWD\src"
python experiments/run_wdm_controller_comparison_campaign.py `
  --output-dir outputs/wdm_controller_comparison_v525
```

The local output contains per-cycle records, per-seed and aggregate scorecards,
paired tests, model and dataset lineage, the policy-saturation audit, a report,
and a SHA-256 artifact manifest. Generated experiment outputs remain ignored by
Git, while the predeclared configuration, campaign code and this result record
are versioned. The report SHA-256 from the completed run is
`47375853e6481295c9eeb52e543d181e476ee4973d7f3170aeca4ac54560f7f9`.

## Next gate

Do not tune the current policy on these observed test outcomes. The next
campaign must define a hierarchical or task-constrained policy using only
training/calibration data, freeze its semantics and thresholds, and then
evaluate it on new simulated seeds. In particular, `PURIFY` and `SWAP` should
be available according to topology/task state rather than competing
unconditionally. Only after that remediation should a frozen model bundle be
promoted to dashboard replay.
